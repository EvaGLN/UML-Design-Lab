## Introduction and Context

In this project, you will work in a team to design a software system based on a real-world scenario.

This activity will take place during the **Peer Learning Day (PLD)** and is designed to:

* simulate a real collaborative design session
* expose you to multiple valid design approaches
* develop your ability to justify modeling and design decisions

Unlike previous projects, this problem does **not have a single correct solution**.
Your goal is to propose a **coherent, consistent, and well-reasoned design**.

---

## Organization

* Teams of **2 to 4 students**
* Duration:

  * **First 2/3 of the day** → analysis, design, and preparation
  * **Final 1/3 of the day** → presentations and discussion

Each team will receive a **scenario description at the beginning of the session**.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this project, you should be able to:

* analyze a problem and identify system components
* design a consistent object-oriented model
* define responsibilities across classes
* model system behavior using sequence diagrams
* justify design decisions and trade-offs
* evaluate alternative solutions

---

## General Requirements

* All diagrams must be delivered in **Mermaid format**
* Use consistent naming across all diagrams
* Your design must be **internally consistent**
* All modeling decisions must be **justifiable**
* Avoid unnecessary complexity or over-engineering

---

## Final Notes

This project is not about finding a single correct solution.

It is about:

* making informed design decisions
* understanding their implications
* learning from alternative approaches

During presentations, you are encouraged to:

* compare your solution with others
* identify strengths and weaknesses
* ask questions and challenge assumptions


## Task
### 0. Problem Analysis <a name='subparagraph0'></a>

### Objective

Understand and structure the problem before designing.

### Instructions

Work as a team to:

* identify the main entities in the system
* identify key relationships between them
* identify the main actions (use cases)

You are encouraged to:

* sketch ideas informally before creating diagrams
* discuss multiple alternatives before choosing one

---

### 1. Class Diagram <a name='subparagraph1'></a>

### Objective

Design the structure of the system.

### Instructions

Create a UML class diagram that includes:

* all relevant classes
* attributes
* methods
* relationships
* multiplicities

### Expectations

* Responsibilities should be clearly assigned
* Relationships must reflect the problem domain
* Multiplicities must be justified
* The diagram must support all required use cases

---

### 2. Sequence Diagrams <a name='subparagraph2'></a>

### Objective

Model key interactions in your system using UML sequence diagrams.

### Instructions

You must create **three (3) sequence diagrams** based on the use cases provided in your selected scenario:

* **1 mandatory use case** (specified in the scenario)
* **2 additional use cases of your choice** from the list provided

Each sequence diagram must represent one complete interaction flow.

### Requirements

For each sequence diagram:

* Include all relevant participants
* Represent the interaction step by step
* Use method names consistent with your class diagram
* Ensure the interaction reflects your design decisions

### Expectations

* Each diagram must follow a **clear and logical sequence of interactions**
* Every message should correspond to a **method defined in your class diagram**
* Responsibilities must be **properly distributed between objects**
* The behavior represented must be **consistent with your system design**

---

### 3. Design Justification <a name='subparagraph3'></a>

### Objective

Explain and defend your design.

### Instructions

Prepare a short explanation covering:

* your main design decisions
* how responsibilities are distributed
* how relationships and multiplicities were defined
* what alternatives you considered
* trade-offs in your solution

# Presentation

Each team will present:

* their class diagram
* their sequence diagrams
* their design decisions

## Presentation Guidelines

* Keep your presentation clear and focused
* Explain **why** you made decisions, not only **what** you built
* Be prepared to answer questions such as:
Why did you choose this relationship?
What happens if requirements change?
What alternatives did you consider?
What would you simplify or improve?
